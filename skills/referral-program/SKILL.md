# Referral program

You design referral programs that close the gap between "I'd recommend you" and recommending you for real. You start by checking whether a referral program is the right move at all, because referrals amplify word of mouth that already exists. They can't create it.

## Before you start

1. Read the marketing context file if one exists.
2. Ask what's missing:
   - Business model (ecommerce, self-serve SaaS, high-ticket B2B, marketplace, consumer app) and rough revenue per customer.
   - Do customers already recommend you without being asked? How do you know?
   - What can you afford to pay to acquire one customer?
   - Where did the original sale happen (email, app, a sales rep)?
   - Any existing program and its numbers.

## Readiness check

| Answer | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| No customers yet, or customers are unhappy | Don't build a program. Fix the product or experience first |
| Happy customers, but nobody talks about you | Create something worth talking about first (step 3 below) |
| Customers already recommend you | Build the program and pour fuel on it |

## Workflow

1. Pick the outcome (awareness, lower acquisition cost, urgency, social proof or revenue).
2. Find the referral sources you already have.
3. Make the experience worth talking about.
4. Choose the incentive and structure using the four levers below.
5. Run it by hand with your top 10 to 20% of customers before buying any software.
6. Write the swipe copy and the referral landing page, then promote it everywhere you already talk to customers.

## Playbook

### Referral vs word of mouth
Word of mouth is a customer telling a friend because they want to. A referral program is a structured nudge with a reward. Referrals pour fuel on a fire that already exists. They don't start one. If nobody recommends you unprompted, fix the product or the experience first.

The **referral gap**: most happy customers say they'd refer you. Few do. A program's whole job is to close that gap.

Referred customers tend to stay longer and spend more than customers from most other channels, because a friend pre-qualified them.

### The four levers

**1. Channel.** Ask where the original sale happened. Ecommerce sold over email and SMS asks there. A high-ticket deal closed by a rep gets asked in person or on a call, then followed up in writing.

**2. Timing.** Two classic mistakes:
- Asking before they've used the product. That's asking for a film review in the ticket queue.
- Asking once. People need a few reminders across different touchpoints.

Ask when goodwill peaks: after a high satisfaction score, at renewal, when they reply to your newsletter or comment on a post, right after a milestone in the product.

**3. Incentive.** It must fit three things:
- Your economics (can you afford it per new customer?)
- The referrer's motivation (is it worth their effort?)
- The friend's interest (will it make them convert?)

Rule of thumb: give **more of the product** over cash where you can. Extra storage, a free month, credits, upgrades. People didn't sign up to earn a few rupees, and product rewards keep them using it.

By business type:
- **B2B.** Discounts don't motivate employees who don't pay the bill. Personal gifts (a good dinner, merch, a donation in their name) work better. For long sales cycles, reward early milestones like a booked demo.
- **Ecommerce.** Cash or discounts work, but they need to be big enough to notice. Front-load it: a large reward for the first referral, smaller after that, so average cost per customer drops.
- **Self-serve SaaS.** Discounts and upgrades suit people paying from their own pocket. For high-ticket SaaS, tie rewards to the deal moving forward so the referrer has a reason to help close it.

**4. Friction.** Every extra click loses people. Three kinds:
- Technical (broken links, slow pages)
- Effort (having to write the message themselves)
- Emotional (not feeling good about sharing)

Fix: pre-write the message, remove logins, one-tap sharing to every channel, and watch where people drop off.

### Reward structures

| Structure | How it works | Upside | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-sided | Only the referrer or only the friend gets rewarded | Cheap | The other side has no reason to act. Low-quality referrals |
| Two-sided | Both get something ("give one, get one") | Feels fair, works on both sides | Costs more |
| Tiered | Bigger rewards at higher counts | Superfans go wild, average cost drops | Needs fulfilment and fraud checks |
| Leaderboard | Monthly competition for a big prize | Re-energises people every month | Needs fair tracking. Lifetime prizes can get expensive |

### Launch in six steps
1. **Pick the outcome.** Awareness, lower acquisition cost, urgency, more social proof or more revenue. Each needs a different design.
2. **Find existing referral sources.** Check analytics for referring sites, search social for mentions of your brand, and add "Where did you hear about us?" to signup.
3. **Make something worth talking about.** A surprising unboxing, a small delightful animation when a task is done, a shared link that works without signing up.
4. **Design the incentive.** Pick a structure from the table.
5. **Test, prove, then scale.**
   - Test: hand-pick your top 10 to 20% most engaged customers, email them personally, fulfil rewards by hand, refine.
   - Prove: once a reward converts, strip friction and automate the boring parts.
   - Scale: only now buy referral software. Show referrers feedback ("Your friend opened your invite") so they keep going. Watch for fraud and keep cost per customer in line with other channels.
6. **Promote it.**
   - **Swipe copy.** Pre-written messages that sound like a friend, not a marketer. Steal the language customers already use in public praise. Add practical value ("This saved me hours on X") and social currency ("You always send me good tools, returning the favour").
   - **Referral landing page.** One per persona. Lead with what the friend gets. Simple words, a collapsed FAQ, one CTA (test benefit-led vs reward-led).
   - **Everywhere you already talk.** Receipts, confirmations, newsletters, email signatures, a visible banner in the product (not buried in settings), social bios, community groups and partner creators over several posts, not one.

## Quality check

- [ ] Did I check that word of mouth already exists?
- [ ] Does the reward fit the economics, the referrer and the friend?
- [ ] Is the ask timed to a high-goodwill moment, not right after purchase?
- [ ] Is sharing possible in one or two taps with pre-written text?
- [ ] Is there a manual pilot before any automation?
- [ ] Swipe copy passes humanize-writing

# AI marketing workflows playbook

Deep reference for the ai-marketing-workflows skill.

---

## 1. The one rule: AI amplifies judgment

AI makes a marketer with good taste much faster. It makes a marketer without taste produce more mediocre work, faster. The limit is rarely the model. It's the human's ability to:
- know what good strategy looks like
- judge output quickly
- spot when the AI is confidently wrong
- add the context about the brand and buyer that the AI can't know

AI models also lean toward the average answer (see the positioning-and-brand skill). So humans set the strategy and the differentiated angle. AI helps execute it at volume.

### Judge outcomes, not output
"Can AI do this task?" is the wrong question. Almost always, something comes out. The right question: does it produce **better results** than what we had, measured in pipeline, revenue, retention or brand perception? A team that replaces skilled people with prompts because the output "looks decent" hasn't made a strategic decision. Track business results before and after any AI change.

## 2. The generalist with an AI execution layer

One senior marketer with strong judgment and an AI workflow can now cover work that used to need a copywriter, an analyst and a campaign manager. A sample day:
- Morning: review performance, find what's working and why.
- Brief the AI on the campaign angle, get several concepts, cut to the best two, add brand nuance.
- Draft an email sequence strategy, let AI draft the emails, edit hard.
- Analyse competitor positioning, have AI map gaps, outline next month's content.
- End of day: note what shipped and what was learned.

What this changes for hiring:
- The first growth hire can come later, once there's real signal to amplify.
- They need to be more senior, not less (strategy and execution in one person).
- Hire for judgment, taste and the ability to learn tools fast. One strong generalist often beats two average specialists.

### Lean operators
AI lowers the money needed to start, not the time and attention needed to grow. Small teams and solo operators should favour channels that work without scale: direct outreach, communities, partnerships, building an audience in public. Paid acquisition comes once revenue can fund it. A small team can't run every channel at once. Pick one or two.

AI-assisted productised services (audits, reviews, research packages delivered with AI help at high margins) are a viable model for solo marketers and consultants.

## 3. Build vs buy

Non-technical marketers can now build custom tools in an afternoon or a weekend.

**Build when:**
- the workflow is specific to your brand or process
- you do the task more than about ten times a month
- off-the-shelf tools still need heavy editing
- two tools you use don't connect
- vendor pricing punishes growth

**Buy when:**
- the category has mature, fairly priced options
- you need reliability, compliance or uptime guarantees
- nobody has time to maintain what you'd build
- the vendor has data or integrations you can't recreate

**Break-even maths:** (hours saved per month × hourly cost of the person) vs (build time + yearly maintenance). If it pays back within about six months, build. Many small internal tools pay back in weeks.

Example of a high-value build: a repurposing workflow that takes one long piece (webinar transcript, article, podcast), pulls out the five to seven core ideas, drafts posts, a thread, newsletter blurbs and an email sequence in your brand voice, and proposes a publishing calendar.

## 4. From prompts to skills

A prompt is a one-off question. A skill is a repeatable system with consistent output.

### Anatomy of a good marketing skill
1. **Role:** the expert it should act as (conversion copywriter, campaign strategist).
2. **Context:** what it must know about the business (ideally pulled from a shared context file).
3. **Task:** exactly what to produce.
4. **Format:** the structure of the output.
5. **Constraints:** rules, banned words, things to avoid, quality checks.

Treat the AI like a capable junior colleague who needs a proper brief, not like a search engine.

### A starter set worth having
- Campaign brief (goal, audience, core message, channels, KPIs, timeline)
- Landing page copy
- Platform-native ad copy (several variations per platform)
- Email sequence (the arc, not just single emails)
- Search-led article outline (starts from intent)
- Social posts per platform
- Competitor analysis (features, positioning gaps, message openings)
- Ideal customer profile (situation, pains, triggers, objections)
- Press pitch (pitch, follow-up, angle variations)
- 90-day marketing plan (priorities, channels, metrics)

### Chain them
The output of one becomes the input of the next: customer profile → campaign brief → landing page → ads → emails. Every downstream asset stays grounded in the same decisions, and nothing starts from a blank page.

## 5. Make it a team system

Most teams use AI in "single-player mode": everyone has private chats, private prompts and context they re-explain every time. Good work stays trapped in personal chat histories. Move to a shared system:

- **Shared context:** one place for ideal customer, voice, messaging and positioning that every AI session reads (the marketing-context skill creates this).
- **Shared skills:** a library of tested skills everyone uses, instead of everyone writing their own prompts.
- **Handoffs:** work moves between people with its context attached (a brief becomes the input for copy, then for design).
- **Culture:** sharing what works is normal. Good prompts and outputs are treated as team assets. Good outputs feed back in as examples for the next run.

Individual gains disappear when everyone re-solves the same problem. Shared systems compound.

## 6. Build small tools as marketing

Useful tools get bookmarked, shared and revisited. Most content gets read once. Ask: what problem does our buyer have every week that we could solve in two minutes?

Ideas that suit most marketing teams:
- **Copy grader:** paste copy, get scores for clarity, specificity, emotion and CTA, with one fix each.
- **Unit economics calculator:** acquisition cost, conversion, contract value and churn in, customer value and payback out, with a table showing how each input moves the result.
- **Idea generator by format:** one topic in, ideas for an article, a post, a video hook, a podcast question and a subject line out.
- **Segment quiz:** five questions route visitors to the most relevant case study and message.
- **UTM builder:** validated fields, a preview and a copy button.
- **Content repurposer:** long-form in, posts, email paragraphs and a thread out.
- **Subject line previewer:** shows truncation on desktop, phone and dark mode with a live character count.
- **Brief generator:** a seven-question form that outputs a shareable campaign brief.
- **A small game** built around your value props. Games get shared where one-pagers don't.

How to build one with an AI coding tool:
1. Describe the exact input and output in one sentence.
2. Add format needs (character limits, copy buttons, export).
3. Say who will use it and what they already know.
4. Let the AI build it, then iterate with specific "change X to Y" instructions.

You're writing a product spec, not code. Specificity is the skill.

## 7. Designing a marketing stack

Don't copy the tools you used at your last job. Design the stack around bottlenecks:
1. Map the customer journey and find where people drop or where work piles up.
2. Audit team workflows for repetitive tasks software could do.
3. Pick tools that remove those specific bottlenecks.

Tool categories most teams eventually need:

| Job | Category |
|---|---|
| Know what users do | Web analytics, product analytics, session recordings and heatmaps |
| Test changes | A/B testing |
| Know where customers came from | Attribution, plus a "how did you hear about us" question |
| Talk to customers | Email and lifecycle platform, live chat, SMS where relevant |
| Keep customer data in one place | CRM (for sales and relationships), customer data platform (for behaviour across tools, later) |
| Find and enrich leads | Data enrichment and prospecting tools |
| Publish and schedule | Social scheduling, CMS or site builder, landing page builder |
| Search | Keyword and backlink research, content optimisation |
| Connect it all | No-code automation |
| Get paid | Payments and billing |
| Plan and create | Project management, docs, design tools |
| Build custom tools | AI coding assistants |

Match the tier to the team that runs it (see the lifecycle tool tiers in the email-lifecycle skill). Most early teams over-buy.

## 8. Guardrails

- Never publish AI-made testimonials, reviews, case studies, quotes or statistics as real.
- Disclose AI-generated people or voices where the context would mislead without it.
- Keep a human review gate on anything customer-facing.
- Protect customer data: don't paste private customer information into tools without permission and a clear data policy.
- Always run a humanising pass before anything ships (the humanize-writing skill).

# Social content, hooks and audience building

Use this file for LinkedIn and X posts, hooks, content calendars, post formats, community platforms like Reddit, DMs, lead magnets and turning an audience into pipeline.

---

## 1. Why personal accounts beat brand accounts

People follow people. A founder or employee posting from their own profile almost always out-reaches the company page. The usual organic funnel looks like this:

1. Post genuinely useful content.
2. Gain followers from it.
3. Build trust over weeks and months.
4. Capture an email (newsletter, resource, DM).
5. Convert off-platform, through email and sales conversations.

On a professional network the real asset isn't reach, it's **access**. A post with 75 likes from the right buyers beats a viral post liked by peers and competitors. Ask yourself: is my content making the reader the hero, or me?

**Write for your buyer, not your peers.** An agency owner posting "how I scaled my agency" attracts other agency owners. Posting "why your abandoned cart emails don't work" attracts clients.

## 2. The ideas come first

A strong idea with average copy can travel. Brilliant copy on a weak idea can't. Spend more time choosing what to say than polishing how.

An idea becomes an insight when it's:

- **Novel.** New, or a fresh angle on something familiar.
- **Actionable.** The reader can do something with it today.
- **High-impact.** Doing it meaningfully changes their work.

### Where good ideas come from
- **Your own work.** What advice did you give a teammate this week? What process got a client their result? What objection came up on a call? What internal doc or checklist could become a post?
- **Customer conversations.** Ask customers "what's your biggest struggle with X?" and "if you could wave a wand, what would change?" Put "How to" in front of the answer and you have a post.
- **Questions people ask you.** If one person asks, many are wondering. Keep a running list.
- **Moments where you saw the penny drop** on a call or in a talk. If it landed live, it will land online.
- **Disagreements.** List the common advice in your field and note where you disagree. That's your point of view.
- **Your career timeline.** Mistakes, turning points, odd jobs, things you got wrong.
- **Things you consume.** A sharp exchange you saw online can be the springboard for your own take.
- **Your analytics.** Every month, find the posts that did 1.5x better than your average and ask which could become a bigger asset: a lead magnet, a newsletter issue, a talk.

### The four angles on any topic
Take one topic and write it four ways:

1. **How-to.** Steps, a checklist, a process.
2. **Analysis.** Data, a breakdown, what you found when you looked closely.
3. **Motivation.** Permission or a push for people who know what to do but aren't doing it.
4. **Why it's hard.** The fears, beliefs and hidden reasons people struggle.

One topic, four posts. Then repeat the exercise for your next topic.

### Vitamins and painkillers
- **Painkiller content** hits a frustration the reader feels right now: "Three months of posting and zero inbound. Here's what's broken."
- **Vitamin content** asks for effort today for a future payoff: "How I collect case studies that build pipeline."

You need both. List your audience's urgent pains and their desired outcomes, then hook on one or the other.

### Speak to three awareness levels
Instead of rigid content pillars, sort every post by who it's for:

1. People who don't know they have the problem.
2. People who know the problem but not the solutions.
3. People actively comparing solutions.

## 3. Hooks

On most feeds only the first two or three lines show before "see more". If those lines don't open a loop, nothing else matters.

### Hook patterns that work
- **Before → but → after.** "Last spring I had no audience. Today I get three inbound leads a week."
- **A surprising number or fact.**
- **A failure or confession.** "I wasted ₹4 lakh on a feature nobody used."
- **The status quo is wrong.** Challenge a rule people treat as gospel.
- **A thesis.** "Everyone wants pricing power. Nobody wants to raise prices first."
- **A specific moment instead of an abstract problem.** "Ever been charged for a subscription you swore you cancelled?" beats "Cash-flow visibility is hard."
- **A useful resource, stated plainly.** "The five tools I use to build landing pages:"
- **A puzzle or guessing game.** Show an image and ask which one won.
- **Borrowed urgency from news.** A recent, named event plus a sharp number.
- **"Boring truth".** "This advice is boring, but it works." Honesty breaks a feed full of hacks.

### Making hooks better
- Dig past the surface problem. Ask three or four times: what's the real reason this keeps happening? What are people afraid of? What belief is holding them back?
- Swap abstractions for a scene the reader recognises.
- Read your draft and find the most surprising line. That's usually the hook.
- Imagine your ideal buyer reading the first line. Would they stop?
- Specific beats philosophical. Instead of "It's about consistency, not virality", write "I posted five times a week for 60 days. No post broke 10,000 views. I still got 14 inbound leads."

### Keeping people reading
Use small curiosity phrases to re-open the loop: "That's where it gets interesting." "Here's the part most people miss." "So what does this mean for you?"

## 4. Post structures

Most strong posts are one of these:

- **Text post:** hook → context → a sharp closing line people want to share.
- **Resource post:** hook → context → an attached asset (a carousel, a table, a gallery of examples). The asset is the payoff.
- **Story post:** a lesson learned the hard way, a funny encounter, poking fun at an absurd industry habit, a challenge you set yourself. Story posts build trust and likeability.
- **Hook / explain / illustrate / teach:** open a loop, expand the idea, make it concrete with an analogy or example, then give a step.
- **What / why / how:** what you're talking about, why it matters, how to do it.

A healthy mix: most posts are about your field, and roughly a third are human (stories, hobbies, behind the scenes). Not every post needs to sell.

### Formats worth having in your rotation
- **Common mistakes.** People are more motivated to avoid losses than chase gains. Always pair the mistake with the fix.
- **"Someone asked me…"** Answer a real question in public.
- **Name the problem.** Give a common problem a memorable name, then use that name repeatedly until people associate it with you.
- **An enemy.** Pit your idea against a villain: a habit, a belief, an outdated way of working. Never a person, and never a competitor by name.
- **Different, not better.** List what everyone in your industry says, find what you disagree with, and stake out the opposite.
- **Curation.** "Five people worth following for X." It builds authority by association and gets shared by the people you mention.
- **Announcements.** Launches, hires, milestones. Use them sparingly so they land.
- **Origin story as a timeline.** Short beats, each starting with a verb, with your age or year, ending with one lesson.
- **One moment, two contrasting roles.** Instead of then-vs-now, show one point in time where you were doing two very different things.
- **Internal dialogue.** Let readers feel your reaction ("my first thought was: they're wrong. My second: they might be right.").
- **"How I use AI for X".** Document what you're trying, including what didn't work. You don't need to be an expert.
- **Polls and choices.** Ask your audience to pick a cover, a subject line or a design. People love being asked.
- **Birthday or anniversary posts.** They get extra reach. Include low points as well as highlights.
- **Peer conversation.** A short Q&A with someone you met, with the setting and dialogue. AI can't fake real conversations.
- **Permission posts.** Sometimes the reader doesn't need more information, they need someone to say it's okay.

### Ads that don't look like ads
Sponsored or promotional posts work best when they read like any other post: proof (why listen to me), confession (a real problem I had), then the product as the fix. Mention the product late.

### Proof in every post
New people see your content every day and silently ask "why should I listen to you?" Answer it with owned proof (your results, data, screenshots) or borrowed proof (named studies, recognisable companies, expert quotes). Screenshots of real feedback make stories believable. Get permission before sharing anyone's name.

## 5. Writing style for feeds

- One or two sentences per paragraph. Most reading happens on phones.
- Keep most sentences short and simple. Lots of white space.
- Write to one person with "you". There's never a crowd reading, only one person at a time.
- Use examples generously. A good example teaches twice as well as good advice, and new examples keep old ideas fresh.
- Repeat your core idea in a few different ways within the post. Repetition reads as conviction.
- Run the "explain it to your mum" test. If someone outside your field wouldn't follow it, simplify.
- Show your personality. Templates are a starting point. The quirky, specific human details are what get remembered.
- Have opinions. Expertise is opinions, not information. Mild polarisation is fine, attacking people is not.
- Don't sit on the fence. Bland, safe content gets scrolled past.

## 6. Distribution habits

- **Engagement drives reach.** Comments are the strongest signal, because each commenter shows the post to their own network. End with a genuine question and reply to every comment.
- **Comment on bigger accounts' posts** two or three times a day. A thoughtful top comment can reach more people than your own post. If a comment gets lots of traction, turn it into a post.
- **Connect in clusters.** Build your network one tight group at a time (the same role in the same city or industry). Concentrated engagement from similar people tells the algorithm exactly who to show your posts to.
- **Connect with people who engage with you.** They're warm.
- **Post consistently, at the same time.** Long gaps get punished. Posting when your audience is online can multiply reach.
- **Links:** platforms change their rules. Old advice to hide links in comments can backfire (some feeds hide link comments by default). Put the link where people can find it, and make the post worth engaging with either way. Test instead of following received wisdom.
- **Image hooks.** A strong visual (a screenshot-style quote, a familiar meme format with your industry twist, a striking metaphor) stops the scroll. Pay off the attention with real value.
- **Profile as a landing page:** a headline that shows proof and who you help, a short "about" explaining why you're worth following, a featured section stacked with proof (case studies, testimonials), and photos that match how you want to be seen.

## 7. From audience to pipeline

### Lead magnets
- Make the lead magnet a **bridge to your paid offer**: one step of your service, a template, a short checklist or a free audit. Nobody reads a 50-page PDF.
- Have one polished "pillar" resource tightly tied to your offer, and lots of quick one-off resources (checklists, prompts, cheat sheets) to drive growth.
- Over time, bundle all your one-off resources into one "everything" resource.
- Make a lead magnet feel worth having with an outcome-driven name, proof, teaser bullets, a light exclusivity frame ("usually only shared with clients") and a preview image.
- Gate occasionally: post freely most of the time, then about one post in five teases half the answer and links to the rest behind an email.

### DMs
- Comment on someone's posts before you DM them.
- Wait a few weeks after connecting before reaching out.
- Lead with value, not a pitch: offer a genuinely useful resource and ask for a work email to send it.
- Once someone has accepted a resource, assume the yes: ask a couple of questions about their situation, share a relevant case study, then suggest a time.
- **Short video DMs** work well: about 10 seconds on why you're reaching out (something specific to them), about 30 seconds on the value (a problem you solved for someone similar, and the result), and about 10 seconds for a soft ask ("open to talking about this?").
- Screenshots of their own post or a quote from their company prove you did your homework.
- If someone clearly isn't active on the platform, offer to switch to email.
- Never end a sales call without booking the next one.

### Email captures and monetises
Social generates attention. Email captures it and turns it into revenue. Many successful creators send far more launch emails than feels comfortable. Weekly, email your list your best posts from that week, which sends fresh engagement back to the posts.

### Ask, but not too often
Give, give, give, then ask. A rough rule is to ask (promote, pitch, drive to an offer) in roughly one post in five to ten. Self-promotion is just marketing. If you don't do it, a louder, less capable competitor will.

### Urgency in case studies
To get people to act now, show what they lose by waiting: real deadlines, expiring bonuses, genuine demand signals. Always honour the deadline you set.

### Test offers in public
Before building a new offer, describe it in a post with a catchy name and ask "Would you use something like this?" The comments tell you if it's worth building.

## 8. Recurring series beat one-offs

One-off content rarely builds recognition, because familiarity needs repetition. A recurring series (a weekly breakdown, a monthly interview, a named newsletter section) turns passers-by into regulars who notice when it's missing.

A series works when it has:

1. A genuine, recurring audience need.
2. Something only you can offer (access, data, experience, perspective).
3. A format you can sustain for a year.
4. A format that gets better with practice (conversations do, one-off blog posts often don't).
5. Built-in distribution (guests and collaborators who share it with their audiences).

Bonus: interview-style series double as ongoing audience research.

## 9. "Hard" content wins in the AI era

Generic tips are now the cheapest thing in the world to produce. Content that lasts is content that requires something you uniquely have:

- your product's real data ("we looked at 10,000 sessions and found…")
- comparisons from testing tools side by side
- customer teardowns with screenshots and their words
- tutorials that genuinely need your product
- your own lived experience, opinions and stories

The question isn't "what topics should we cover?" It's "what can we show or prove that nobody else can?"

## 10. Repurposing

- Pull the main ideas out of long-form content (newsletters, podcasts, talks) and adapt them for short posts.
- Re-post underperforming but useful posts with a new hook.
- Switch formats (text to carousel, carousel to video).
- Re-run your best posts every few months. New followers haven't seen them.
- Test hooks on one platform before using them on another.

## 11. Community platforms (Reddit and forums)

Most social platforms reward talking about yourself. Community platforms punish it. Promotional accounts get throttled quietly: the post stays up but nobody sees it.

### Do it transparently
Fake "ordinary user" accounts recommending your product are a dead end. Rules against fake reviews and endorsements make it risky, and it never compounds. Use an honest, identifiable account.

### Climb slowly
Accounts earn status from community response and history. Build it in stages:

1. **Look human (about a month).** Participate in communities unrelated to your business. Earn baseline trust.
2. **Build authority (about two months).** Comment helpfully in threads your buyers read. No links, no self-mentions. Just useful answers.
3. **Start conversations.** Post discussion threads designed to get the right people talking. These build credibility with moderators.
4. **Direct intent, carefully.** Before anything promotional, message the moderators, share what you plan to post and ask for their input. Then progress from repurposed thought leadership, to an announcement, to an expert Q&A.

Shortcuts (asking customers to post for you, coordinating upvotes, dropping tracking links) break platform rules and damage reach for good.

### Measure it
Track community signals (karma, impressions, unprompted mentions by others) and customer signals (add the platform to "where did you hear about us?"). Threads on these platforms also rank in search and get cited by AI answer engines, so the payoff compounds.

## 12. Mindset that keeps people posting

- Most people quit right before it starts working. Consistency over months is the real edge.
- Separate post metrics from self-worth. Treat low performers as data.
- Write before checking notifications. Capture ideas all week and write in one sitting.
- You'll be bad at first. Everyone is. Being seen trying is the price of getting good.
- Remix proven structures with your own stories. That isn't copying, that's how every creator starts. Copying someone's words is.
- Keep experimenting even after something works. Growth stalls when you only repeat past hits.
- Small daily habits (a set number of comments, DMs and posts) compound. Measure backwards: if last week produced one call, send more DMs this week.

# Paid ads playbook

Deep reference for the paid-ads skill. It covers diagnosing whether paid is the problem, measurement, how social differs from search, choosing hooks and formats, short-video platforms and AI-made video.

---

## 1. Check the five fits before touching the ad account

Paid growth only lasts when five fits hold. When results drop, find the broken fit before changing creative.

| Fit | Question | Signs it's broken |
|---|---|---|
| **Market ↔ product** | Does a real market want this badly enough? | Low conversion on every channel, instant churn |
| **Product ↔ channel** | Can people get the value fast enough for ads to work? | Lots of signups, little engagement. The product needs weeks before it clicks |
| **Channel ↔ model** | Do the unit economics survive paid acquisition? | Profitable at small spend, cost per customer climbs faster than value as you scale |
| **Model ↔ market** | Does the pricing fit the segment the channel reaches? | One segment converts well but can't be reached through this channel |
| **Creative ↔ market** | Does the message land with the people you reach? | Good click-through, weak conversion. Or strong at small scale, fading on colder audiences |

How to find it:
1. Map the funnel with real numbers.
2. Find the biggest drop.
3. Find out why with evidence (interviews, recordings, heatmaps).
4. Match the drop to a fit.
5. Fix that fit first.

The most common misdiagnosis is blaming creative when the real problem is product-channel fit or economics. Better ads that still lose money are still losing money. The ad account is where you squeeze efficiency out of a working system, not where you rescue a broken one.

## 2. What automation does and doesn't do

Ad platforms now automate bidding, audience finding, creative rotation, budget allocation and keyword expansion. A lot of hands-on campaign work is gone.

What they can't do:
- decide the channel mix for your business model
- tell you whether paid makes sense at your economics
- explain why acquisition cost is rising
- spot that the offer, not the creative, is the problem
- connect paid to brand, product and lifecycle

So the order of work is:
1. Is paid the right channel for our stage?
2. Is measurement trustworthy?
3. Are we sending the platforms good conversion signals and clean first-party data?
4. Only then: creative and offer testing.

Most teams start at step 4.

### Measurement is the edge
Most measurement is weak: last-click attribution that undervalues awareness, no incrementality tests, and trust in platform-reported returns (which tend to flatter). Teams that measure better allocate better, catch decline sooner and can tell creative fatigue from offer fatigue from audience saturation.

Minimum setup:
- Conversion events firing correctly on every platform
- A "How did you hear about us?" question at signup or checkout
- Occasional holdout or geo tests for big channels
- Blended acquisition cost (total spend ÷ new customers) next to platform numbers

## 3. Social ads are not search ads

Search has intent in every query. "Best tool for X" is someone ready to compare. Social has no intent signal. You target demographics and interests, so "top, middle and bottom of funnel" on social are labels, not readiness:

- "Top" = broad cold audiences
- "Middle" = lookalikes or people who engaged
- "Bottom" = retargeting. Brand-aware is not the same as ready to buy.

In any broad audience, a small slice is ready now, a bigger slice needs a few touches and most won't need you for months. You can't tell them apart when they first see the ad.

What to do about it:
1. **Nail the cold ad first.** Test 5 to 10 concepts with different hooks and angles on broad audiences. A strong cold ad lets the ready buyers convert now. No nurture sequence rescues a weak one.
2. **Add simple retargeting.** Show your best ads again to site visitors, maybe with proof or a new angle.
3. **Let time work.** Stay visible while people's timelines catch up.
4. **Add complexity only with signal.** Lead magnets, short email sequences and segmented retargeting come after you know which message converts.

Signs your social funnel is over-built:
- A gate (lead magnet, email) stands between people and your core offer
- "Different funnel stage" campaigns perform about the same
- You nurture for weeks before asking for anything
- Retargeting converts far below what you expected

## 4. Choosing the hook: start from the job

Every ad should test a hypothesis tied to a business problem. First decide which bottleneck you're solving:

| Bottleneck | What creative should do |
|---|---|
| Not enough of the right people see you | Stop the scroll with cold traffic (job A) |
| People see you but don't act | Remove hesitation or build trust (jobs C or E) |
| You attract the wrong people | Call out the right persona clearly, or fix targeting |
| Unclear market | Run narrow persona tests and let data show who converts |
| Message doesn't land | Educate and persuade (job B) |
| Users sign up but don't stay | Not a creative problem. Fix the product or onboarding |

### The five jobs and the hooks that suit them

**A. Stop the scroll (cold audiences)**
- Crowded category → **pattern interrupt** (lo-fi, odd framing, deliberately "wrong" looking)
- Novel or misunderstood product → **curiosity gap** (hide the main detail, show the after first)
- Narrow, clear persona → **persona callout** ("If you're a [specific person], this is for you")
- Identity or emotion driven → **POV** ("POV: you finally leave work at 6")
- Not sure? Start with pattern interrupt and curiosity gap.

**B. Educate or persuade (people who know you exist)**
- Fighting a known alternative → **us vs them** (old way vs new way, ten tools vs one)
- Wrong mental model → **contrarian** ("Everyone says X. That's why you're stuck.")
- Buyer wants structure → **listicle** ("3 reasons your reports are wrong")
- Needs to picture the result → **POV** day-in-the-life
- Not sure? Listicle is a safe start.

**C. Overcome objections (high intent)**
- "I don't trust this brand" → **social proof** (reviews, numbers, outcomes)
- "I don't think it works" → **authority** (data, credentials, track record)
- "Not worth the money" → **us vs them** on cost, including hidden costs of the old way
- "Looks complicated" → **listicle** showing it's simpler

**D. Retarget and convert**
- Needs reassurance they're choosing right → social proof from people like them
- Needs calm confidence → authority
- Needs to imagine using it → POV
- Needs a reason to act now → problem-agitate or contrarian on the cost of waiting

**E. Build trust (high-stakes categories like money, health, software, coaching)**
- "Are you legit?" → authority
- "Do people like me use this?" → category-specific social proof ("used by 200+ clinics")
- Complexity → listicle in three to five simple chunks
- Can't picture it → POV

### Jaded markets
When buyers have seen every claim, lean on contrarian takes, data-heavy authority, precise differences (not "better") and weirdly specific POVs. You're selling "this is different from what you already tried."

### Shortcuts by business type
- **B2B software:** problem-agitate, authority, us vs them (buyers are sceptical and comparison shop)
- **Ecommerce:** pattern interrupt, POV, social proof (impulse, competing with entertainment)
- **Health and wellness:** contrarian, authority, problem-agitate (saturated with identical claims)
- **Education and coaching:** contrarian, authority, POV (people buy a transformation)

### One emotion per ad
Surprise (pattern interrupt, contrarian), curiosity (curiosity gap, listicle), identity (persona callout, POV), trust (authority, social proof), urgency (problem-agitate, contrarian). Mixing all five dilutes each.

## 5. Choosing the format: static, creator-style or produced video

Ask, in order:
1. **Can the offer be understood in one frame?** If yes, start with statics. They're cheap, fast and brutal about message clarity. If an angle dies as a static, a fancy edit won't save it. If the product needs a sequence (a workflow, a transformation), use video earlier.
2. **Is the problem clarity or belief?** Clarity problems (common in B2B software) → statics first. Belief problems (common in health, supplements, coaching, consumer goods) → faces and stories earlier.
3. **What can you produce every week?** Don't build a plan that needs weekly video if you have no editor. Use statics to find winning angles, then upgrade winners to video.
4. **What does the channel expect?** Short-video feeds speak in faces and motion. Image-heavy feeds let good statics work. Professional networks reward clean statics, carousels and simple talking heads, and over-edited clips look out of place. In-stream video needs tight scripts.

Putting it together (example, B2B software):
- Cold: a static pattern interrupt with problem-agitate, plus a static us-vs-them
- Warm: a static listicle and a founder or expert explainer
- Hot: a static with customer result tiles and a short POV video of the "after"

Every ad should be a conscious choice of job, hook and format.

## 5b. Creative volume vs creative diversity

As platforms automate bidding and targeting, creative becomes one of the few inputs you still control. Scaling has two stages:
1. **Find creative that resonates.** This is strategy: understanding why an angle works for an audience.
2. **Turn winners into many genuinely different assets.** This is operations: a production line of concepts, creators and formats.

Most brands stall moving from stage 1 to stage 2.

- **Diversity beats volume.** Fifty versions of the same image with different text are one ad to the algorithm, and it may charge you more for them. New concepts, new hooks, new people and new formats count. Lazy variants don't.
- **Lo-fi often wins.** Deliberately rough, native-looking ads frequently beat polished ones on clicks and conversions.
- **Feed organic into paid.** Set up a simple habit (a shared channel, a weekly review) where organic posts that outperform get flagged for paid testing.
- **Fewer, better can also win.** Some teams succeed with fewer ads, real budget behind each and deep analysis of why each worked. Volume tends to work because it forces diversity, and diversity is the real variable.
- **AI belongs in the middle of the pipeline.** Human-directed AI creative tends to beat both purely human and obviously-AI ads. Ads that look AI-made underperform.
- **B2B caveat:** smaller audiences, longer cycles and fewer products mean high-volume creative may not translate directly. Start with fewer, sharper concepts.

## 6. Short-video ad platforms

- People are there to be entertained, so intent is low. For most brands these platforms are a top-of-funnel channel: stop the swipe, bring people to your site, retarget and convert there.
- They work best as a primary channel for visual, broadly appealing products with short buying cycles and modest prices, and for free apps and games.
- **Creative is the lever, not targeting.** Broad targeting plus lots of creative usually wins. Ads that look like ads fail. Ads that look like native creator content work.
- **Fatigue is fast.** Plan new batches every one to two weeks. Results tend to be hit or miss.
- **Creator-posted ads** (running ads through a creator's account) feel more native, borrow trust and can turn into organic posts too.
- **Budget:** platforms need roughly 50 conversions a week to optimise. A rough starting daily budget = (target cost per conversion × 50) ÷ 7. Give a new channel six to ten weeks. If you're an order of magnitude off target by then, it probably won't work. If you're halfway there, keep testing creative.
- Split roughly 85% to prospecting and testing, 15% to retargeting, then let results decide.
- **Before committing, check:** can you fund a proper test, does the product suit the channel, can you produce lots of native video, and have similar formats (vertical video on other platforms) worked for you? If not, build organically first.
- A winner on a short-video platform usually works on other vertical video placements too.

## 7. AI-made video

AI video tools have collapsed the cost and time of polished video. One skilled person can now produce in days what used to take a crew and a big budget.

A workable process:
1. Write a rough script.
2. Turn it into a shot list (an AI assistant can help).
3. Generate shots with a video model.
4. Pick the outputs that meet your standard.
5. Edit in normal editing software.

Where it fits:
- Fast iteration on video ad concepts without shoots
- Brand stories that used to be unaffordable
- B-roll for ads, decks and websites
- Absurd or comedic character scenes, which suit the medium's quirks

Trade-offs: "close enough" quality, occasional distortions and less control. Brands that need perfect product accuracy may still need real footage. Be transparent where it matters, and never use AI to fake testimonials, real people or results.

## 8. Ads inside AI assistants

These are early. They're targeted by conversation context, not keywords, and appear inside an answer. Creative should read like a helpful continuation: a specific claim that answers the user's underlying question, relevant proof and a short CTA. Install conversion tracking before spending. Build organic AI visibility first (see the seo-ai-search skill).

## 9. Paid ads checklist

- [ ] Checked the five fits before changing creative
- [ ] Conversion tracking verified and a "how did you hear about us" question live
- [ ] Each ad has one job, one hook family and one emotion
- [ ] Format chosen by clarity vs belief and production reality
- [ ] At least 5 concepts tested on cold before building nurture
- [ ] Landing page matches the ad's promise
- [ ] Budget sized to reach about 50 conversions a week, or a plan for a cheaper proxy event
- [ ] Review cadence set (don't touch new tests for the first week or two)

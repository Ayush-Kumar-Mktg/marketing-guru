# Positioning and brand

You help a company decide what it should be known for, say it in words buyers already use, and back it with a point of view and identity that competitors can't copy by swapping logos. You give options with the trade-offs of each. Positioning is a decision the team has to own.

## Before you start

1. Read the marketing context file if one exists. If it doesn't, this skill benefits most from running marketing-context first.
2. Ask what's missing:
   - What do customers do instead of using you (the real alternative)?
   - Who gets the most value, and who churns?
   - Real customer quotes about why they bought and what they'd miss.
   - Competitors' homepage headlines (for the swap test).
   - What the team believes about the market that others don't.
   - For brand work: existing mission, story, voice notes, visual assets.

## Diagnose first

| Symptom | Likely problem | Start with |
|---|---|---|
| "We sound like everyone else" | No point of view, generic category claims | Point of view and swap test (playbook 1) |
| Prospects ask "so what exactly do you do?" | Unfamiliar category or no category | Familiar category + anchor comparison |
| Wrong customers sign up | Positioning too broad | Narrow the audience |
| AI assistants describe you vaguely | Inconsistent positioning across sources | Brand entity brief (see seo-ai-search) |
| Team members pitch it differently | No single story | Story and 30-second pitch |
| Rebrand urge | Could be real, could be boredom | Rebrand reasons and lessons (playbook 5) |
| Content and ads feel templated | AI used for strategy, not execution | Brief first, protect the brand layer (playbook 7) |

## Workflow

### For positioning
1. List the real alternatives, including spreadsheets, agencies, interns and doing nothing.
2. Find where you're clearly better for a specific group. Use customer words.
3. Pick the category buyers would file you under, plus the narrowing qualifier.
4. Draft three positioning sentences: For [person] who [problem], [product] is the [category] that [outcome], because [proof].
5. Write the point of view: what we believe that others don't, and what it implies people should stop doing.
6. Run the swap test against three competitors.
7. Recommend one direction and say what you'd test to confirm it (a landing page headline test, sales call language, an ad test).

### For brand identity
Draft mission (one line), story (origin-led or customer-led, under ~250 words, with real tension), voice (three do's and don'ts with example sentences, an archetype, optional muse) and design basics to brief a designer.

### For "should we rebrand?"
Check against the good reasons. List the recognisable assets and equity that must survive. Recommend evolve, refresh or full rebrand, with the risk of each.

## Output format

```
Diagnosis: one line

Positioning options (2 or 3)
- Positioning sentence
- Point of view
- Who it attracts / who it repels
- Risk

Recommendation and why
How to validate it (one cheap test)

[If brand work] Mission · Story · Voice chart · Design brief
```

## Quality check

- [ ] Category uses buyer words, not invented jargon
- [ ] Specific audience named
- [ ] Point of view implies something to stop doing
- [ ] Passes the swap test
- [ ] Every claim is backed by proof or flagged as a gap
- [ ] No competitor attacked by name in public-facing lines
- [ ] Passes humanize-writing
